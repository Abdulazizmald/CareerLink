One cause, two separate data problems. The worst offender is a clothing retailer posting a checkout supervisor job 452 times. That inflated the posting count, and because "front end" reads as technical, it also put a retail role into the software engineering family. The taxonomy fix removes the misfiling; only counting distinct adverts removes the inflation. Neither fix alone was enough.

5. The level label is missing at scale

Seniority is read from the job title and never from the description, because job ads are full of "lead a team" and "reports to the manager". The cost of that decision is {{ missing.total|count }} postings with no level, and the cost is not spread evenly.

{{ dots(level_rows, 60, "%") }}

Over half of IT support postings carry no level. {% if missing.exceeding %}And in {{ missing.exceeding|join(", ") }}, unlabelled postings show a higher median than ones labelled senior, which is the signature of a missing label rather than a real level. It is the only family where that happens, so this is one anomaly and not a general pattern.{% endif %} This is the case for training a model to read the level out of the description text: the label comes from the title, the evidence comes from the body, and a third of the market is currently unreachable by any filter.
